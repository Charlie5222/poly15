The app paper-bets every round with a pretend bankroll and keeps score. Leave it running, then come back to a report: profit, win rate, and which conditions it did well or badly in. No real money moves.

What it found

Conditions where the BUY call did better or worse than its break-even. With dozens of slices, a few will look good by luck, so "could still be luck" means exactly that.

Every pattern it checked

The BUY call's bets split every way that might matter. The bar is the win rate; the tick is what it needed.

Every-minute test

At each whole minute left in a round, the call is written down as a pretend bet. These are kept apart from the totals above, and the model doesn't learn from them. The bar is the win rate; the tick is what it needed.

Exchange gap test

The rule, fixed in advance: when the exchanges sit $20 or more from the settlement index, pretend-bet $10 the way they point, at every whole minute left and at 30 seconds. Prices above 97¢ are skipped. Kept apart from the totals above.

Faster price check

The settlement index behaves like a rolling average, so exchange moves take time to feed into it. "Ahead by" is how long that lasts.

Selling when the call flips

Instead of holding a bet to the end, sell it at the next check if the call has switched sides.

Is it beating the market?

Each settled round, the model's forecast and the market's are both scored on what happened. The bar fills as evidence builds that the model forecasts better. It passes at 100%.

Does it mean what it says?

When it says 70%, it should win about 70% of the time. Dots near the line are honest.

What it pays attention to

How far each input pushes its forecast away from the market's. Green leans Up, red leans Down.
